In progress Auto workshop Germany

Rossack Auto

A real client project, currently based in Germany — shown here as an example of my work while I build up an Australian portfolio. A family-run auto workshop: decades in the same suburb, a reputation built almost entirely on word of mouth — and a website that showed none of it. The job here isn't to make the workshop look better than it is. It's to make it finally look the way its customers already describe it.

Currently being built: five pages, one clear path to a booking request, and a services list that still makes sense to someone who doesn't know what a wheel alignment is. Alongside it, a Google Business Profile that matches the website — photos, opening hours and services all maintained in one place.

  • Google Business Profile
  • Ongoing support

Concept Wine bar & tapas

Blush & Wine

A concept for a wine bar that comes alive by candlelight in the evening — and whose website still has to work at midday. The design picks up the warm half-dark of the room instead of drowning it out with a white page.

At the centre: the menu. Not as a PDF you pinch and zoom on your phone, but as its own page that can be updated in thirty seconds when a wine runs out. Around it, table bookings, opening hours and events.

Concept Physiotherapy

Nordlight Physiotherapy

A concept for a physiotherapy clinic. People who land on a site like this are usually in pain and short on patience — so booking sits at the top, not the bottom, and "can I claim on my health fund?" gets answered before anyone has to go looking for it.

Calm colours, plenty of breathing room, and large type for patients whose eyes aren't what they used to be. Plus a team section with real names and specialties — when it comes to your health, you choose people, not clinics.

  • Medicare, health funds & HICAPS

Concept Burger bar

Black Market

A concept for an urban, late-night burger joint that isn't trying to be anyone's friendly neighbourhood spot. Burgers, loaded fries and shakes — nothing else, no apology for it. Matte black, one red accent, copy that reads like a dare: "We don't do salads."

The centrepiece is a scroll-driven build sequence — a burger assembles itself layer by layer as you scroll, bun to patty to cheese to sauce, then drops into a box next to the fries and a shake. It's the technical showcase piece: the kind of animation that only earns its place if it stays smooth on an ordinary phone, not just in a demo reel.

  • Scroll-driven build animation
  • Full menu with pricing
  • Mobile-first layout
  • Location & hours

For context

Real client work, and where it's concept.

I could put a row of logos up here and hope nobody asks which ones are real. Plenty of people do. I think that's a poor start to a working relationship built on you trusting me.

So: Rossack Auto is a real client project, currently in progress for a workshop based in Germany — shown here, with the client's permission, while I build up a portfolio of Australian work. Alec Wells, Business Coach is a real client project too, also in progress. Blush & Wine, Black Market and Nordlight Physiotherapy are concepts — not real clients. I designed them because I wanted to show how I think about a wine bar, a burger joint and a physio clinic: which question needs answering first in each industry, and how that flows through to colour, type and structure.

What you can see in all of them is the same thing: structure, care, and the decision that a website gets built for the people using it — not just for the business that owns it. That part doesn't change, real client or concept.
